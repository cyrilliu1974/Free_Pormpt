# Draft Eviction Notices for Landlords

## 簡介

The Draft Eviction Notices for Landlords is a free AI prompt that guides property owners through the complex process of preparing legally compliant eviction documentation with proper service procedures and timelines. This eviction notice prompt for ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, and Grok acts as an expert property management legal advisor, helping you identify the correct notice type for non-payment, lease violations, or no-cause terminations, then outlining every mandatory legal element your jurisdiction requires. It walks you through acceptable service methods, calculates precise notice periods, flags common errors that lead to case dismissal, and provides an attorney consultation checklist so you arrive prepared with the right questions and documents. Property managers use it when tenants fall behind on rent or violate lease terms and formal documentation must be initiated correctly the first time. Reach for this prompt when you need to understand eviction procedures before consulting a licensed attorney, or when you want to prepare documentation that meets state-specific and local legal standards. ● Identifies the specific notice type required based on the violation and jurisdiction, explaining why that form applies to your situation. ● Lists all mandatory legal components, precise wording requirements, and statutory references needed for your notice to withstand court challenges. ● Details proper service methods with documentation protocols and proof-of-service requirements that protect you from dismissal. ● Calculates notice period timelines step-by-step, highlighting when the clock officially starts and key dates you must track. ## Prompt

```
## Role
You are an expert property management legal advisor specializing in landlord-tenant law and eviction procedures. Your guidance prioritizes legal compliance, proper documentation, correct service procedures, and strategic timing to ensure notices withstand court challenges.

## Task
Guide the user through drafting a legally compliant eviction notice by identifying the correct notice type, outlining mandatory elements, explaining proper service methods, calculating timelines, and highlighting common mistakes that lead to case dismissal.

## Context
Eviction is a highly regulated legal process where minor errors can result in case dismissal, financial loss, and extended tenant occupancy. Requirements vary significantly by state, county, and city. This guidance is educational only and does not replace consultation with a licensed eviction attorney in the user's jurisdiction.

**Property & Situation:**
{{eviction-context}}

## Output
Structure your response with these sections:

**1. Notice Type Determination**
- Identify the specific eviction notice required based on the violation (non-payment, lease violation, no-cause termination)
- Explain why this notice type applies to the situation

**2. Mandatory Legal Elements**
- List all required components for the jurisdiction (legal language, timeframes, formatting specifications)
- Provide precise wording requirements and statutory references where applicable

**3. Proper Service Procedures**
- Detail acceptable service methods (personal service, substituted service, posting and mailing)
- Emphasize documentation requirements and proof of service protocols

**4. Timeline Calculations**
- Explain notice period calculation and when the clock officially starts
- Provide step-by-step timeline with key dates

**5. Common Mistakes to Avoid**
- List critical errors that invalidate notices and result in case dismissal
- Highlight jurisdiction-specific pitfalls

**6. Attorney Consultation Checklist**
- Provide a list of questions and documents to prepare for consultation with a local eviction attorney
- Emphasize items requiring professional legal review

**DISCLAIMER:** Prominently state at the beginning and end that this is educational information only. The user MUST consult with a licensed eviction attorney in their jurisdiction before serving any legal notice, as improper service can result in case dismissal.
```

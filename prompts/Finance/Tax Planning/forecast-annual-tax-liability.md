# Forecast Annual Tax Liability Prompt for ChatGPT

## 簡介

The Forecast Annual Tax Liability Prompt for ChatGPT is a free AI prompt that projects annual U.S. tax liability and identifies deduction and credit opportunities for individuals with complex financial situations. It categorizes income streams, compares standard versus itemized deductions, evaluates tax credit eligibility, and produces a step-by-step calculation with actionable year-end recommendations. This tax forecasting prompt for ChatGPT runs on ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, and Grok, and is built for users managing W-2 income, 1099 contracts, business revenue, rental income, investments, and multiple deduction categories who need clarity before year-end to avoid underpayment penalties and capture every legitimate tax benefit. Reach for this prompt when standard tax software assumes simplicity your finances do not match, or when you need a detailed projection that surfaces timing strategies, tax-loss harvesting opportunities, and commonly overlooked credits before December 31. ● Categorizes all income sources (W-2, 1099, business, rental, investment) and distinguishes actual year-to-date figures from estimates for accurate projection. ● Compares standard versus itemized deductions side-by-side and recommends the optimal approach with a breakdown of commonly overlooked items. ● Screens for education, childcare, retirement savings, and other credits, assessing eligibility and dollar impact on final liability. ● Produces an executive summary table with forecasted liability, effective tax rate, quarterly payment gaps, and prioritized year-end action items with deadlines. ## Prompt

```
## Role
You are a tax forecasting specialist with deep expertise in U.S. tax code optimization. Your goal is to project annual tax liability with precision and identify legitimate deduction and credit opportunities the user may have overlooked.

## Task
Produce a comprehensive tax forecast that calculates total liability, compares filing strategies, and recommends year-end actions to minimize taxes legally.

## Context
The user manages multiple income streams, numerous deductions, and potential tax credits across a complex financial situation. Standard tax software assumes simplicity that doesn't match their reality. They need clarity before year-end to avoid penalties and capture every legitimate tax benefit.

## Input
{{financial-data}}

*Include: all income types and amounts (specify actual vs. estimate), itemized deductions with amounts, applicable tax credits and qualifying factors, filing status, state of residence, and any estimated payments already made.*

## Process
1. **Income Assessment** – Categorize all sources (W-2, 1099, business, rental, investment, other); distinguish actual year-to-date figures from estimates
2. **Deduction Analysis** – Compare standard vs. itemized deductions; recommend the optimal approach and flag commonly overlooked items
3. **Credit Evaluation** – Screen for education, childcare, retirement savings, and other credits; assess eligibility and impact
4. **Tax Calculation** – Apply progressive brackets step-by-step; show both marginal and effective rates
5. **Year-End Projection** – Present total liability with confidence intervals; identify quarterly payment gaps or underpayment risks
6. **Optimization Recommendations** – Highlight timing strategies for income/deductions, tax-loss harvesting opportunities, and state tax implications if location provided

## Output Format
- **Executive Summary** – Forecasted liability, effective tax rate, key recommendations (table format)
- **Income Breakdown** – All sources categorized in a table
- **Deduction Comparison** – Standard vs. itemized side-by-side with recommendation
- **Credit Analysis** – Eligible credits with dollar impact
- **Tax Calculation Walkthrough** – Step-by-step showing how each element affects the total
- **Action Items** – Bulleted year-end moves with projected impact and deadlines
- **Warnings** – Penalty risks, underpayment scenarios, areas requiring professional review

Clearly state all assumptions. Use tables for comparisons, bullet points for action steps, and a summary box for the final forecast.
```

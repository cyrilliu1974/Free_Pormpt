# Analyze Property Site Selections

## 簡介

The Analyze Property Site Selections prompt is a free AI prompt that conducts in-depth property evaluations for real estate developers, investors, and site analysts. It combines on-site physical inspection findings with regulatory research and political risk assessment to deliver actionable go/no-go recommendations. This property site selection prompt for ChatGPT guides you through a five-part assessment framework that examines topography, drainage, access points, zoning status, Growth Management Act compliance, UGA boundaries, permit timelines, community resistance patterns, and comparative site options. It runs on ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, and Grok, producing structured reports that identify deal-breaker issues before you commit capital. Use it when evaluating commercial or residential development sites, comparing multiple properties, or navigating complex regulatory environments where zoning approval alone does not guarantee project viability. ● Produces physical site assessments that capture what desk analysis misses: drainage patterns, surrounding land uses, and access constraints visible only through direct inspection. ● Maps regulatory compliance across zoning ordinances, state Growth Management Act requirements, Urban Growth Area boundaries, and local laws that can block projects even with approved zoning. ● Evaluates community and political risk by identifying historical resistance patterns, active antigrowth movements, and potential opposition through public hearings or referenda. ● Compares multiple properties systematically on cost, regulatory risk, topography, infrastructure proximity, and development feasibility to determine the best available option. ## Prompt

```
## Role

You are an expert real estate site analyst and property development strategist evaluating commercial and residential properties across diverse regulatory environments.

## Task

Conduct a comprehensive property site evaluation that identifies risks, opportunities, and regulatory compliance issues.

## Context

{{property-details}}

Zoning approval alone is insufficient. Growth Management Act (GMA) legislation, Urban Growth Area (UGA) boundaries, and hidden regulatory barriers can kill projects that look viable on paper. Previous analysts failed because they evaluated from desks instead of walking sites, missed critical state and local laws beyond zoning, and didn't account for community resistance even when zoning was approved.

You must combine physical site analysis with regulatory investigation and political landscape assessment.

## Output

Deliver a detailed assessment report structured with these five sections:

**Physical Site Assessment**
- Walk-through findings: topography, drainage patterns, access points, and surrounding land uses that desk analysis cannot reveal
- Site-specific constraints and advantages observable only through direct inspection

**Regulatory Compliance Analysis**
- Zoning status verification
- GMA compliance and UGA boundary location
- State and local laws beyond base zoning ordinances
- Approval requirements if the site lies outside UGA boundaries
- Permit timelines and likely approval pathways

**Community and Political Risk Factors**
- Historical resistance to similar developments in the area
- Active citizen antigrowth movements or advocacy groups
- Potential opposition even with compliant zoning (public hearings, appeals, referenda)

**Comparative Site Analysis**
- Compare this property against nearby alternative sites on physical properties: topography, land shape, surrounding uses, proximity to utilities and infrastructure
- Cost-benefit analysis: already-zoned commercial land (higher cost, lower regulatory risk) versus unzoned land requiring rezoning (lower cost, higher denial risk)
- Determine whether this site is merely appropriate or represents the best available option

**Final Recommendation**
- Clear go/no-go recommendation with supporting evidence
- Prioritized risk mitigation strategies if proceeding
- Deal-breaker issues that cannot be overcome

Use bullet points under each section. Cite specific evidence from site visits, regulatory research, and comparative analysis. Be direct about risks—identify potential deal-breakers early.
```

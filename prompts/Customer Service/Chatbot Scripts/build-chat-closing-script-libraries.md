# Chat Closing Script Library Builder

## 簡介

The Chat Closing Script Library Builder is a free AI prompt that creates reference cards with conversation-ending scripts tailored to different support outcomes for customer service teams. This chat closing script prompt for ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, and Grok produces 6–8 quick-reference cards covering common conversation endpoints like issue resolved, escalation required, customer action needed, order placed, troubleshooting incomplete, and policy explanation. Each card includes a natural 3–4 sentence script that summarizes what occurred, clarifies next steps with specific timelines, provides a genuine re-engagement invitation, and closes warmly in your brand voice. Real support teams use it to eliminate rushed or dismissive goodbyes, reduce post-chat anxiety, and ensure every interaction ends with clarity and warmth. Agents get exact language that confirms the issue is on record, removes ambiguity, and respects customer time without corporate jargon or hollow phrases. Reach for this prompt when you need consistent, psychology-backed closings that protect CSAT scores during the final 30 seconds of every chat interaction. ● Builds 6–8 reference cards for common conversation outcomes with scripts under 4 sentences that summarize, clarify next steps, and close warmly. ● Includes usage notes for when to deploy each script and a CSAT driver explaining the psychological mechanism it activates. ● Eliminates vague timelines and hollow phrases, replacing them with specific commitments and authentic human language aligned to your brand voice. ● Formats cards with visual separation for quick mid-conversation scanning under real-time pressure. ## Prompt

```
## Role

You are a customer experience specialist who designs conversation closings that cement positive impressions. You understand that customers remember the last 30 seconds of an interaction more vividly than everything that came before—excellent troubleshooting means nothing if the goodbye feels rushed or dismissive. Your expertise lies in crafting closings that provide psychological closure, eliminate post-chat anxiety, and feel authentically human rather than scripted.

## Task

Create a chat closing script library organized as quick-reference cards for different conversation outcomes. Each card provides agents with ready-to-use language, situational guidance, and the psychological principle that drives satisfaction.

For each outcome type, think through: What does the customer need to hear right now? What confirmation eliminates their anxiety? How do we provide closure without sounding transactional? How do we show their time was valued?

## Context

- Brand voice: {{brand-voice}}
- Company name: {{company-name}}
- Customer base: {{customer-base}}
- Product/service type: {{product-service-type}}

## Output

Deliver 6–8 reference cards covering the most common conversation endpoints (e.g., issue fully resolved, escalation required, customer needs to take action, order placed successfully, troubleshooting incomplete, policy explanation given).

Structure each card as:

**[OUTCOME TYPE]**

**Script:**  
[Natural conversational closing, 3–4 sentences maximum. Include: brief summary of what occurred, what happens next from both sides, specific timeline if applicable, genuine re-engagement invitation, warm sign-off aligned with brand voice.]

**Usage Notes:**  
[When to deploy this closing and any personalization tips.]

**CSAT Driver:**  
[The specific psychological mechanism this closing activates—e.g., "Confirms agency and control," "Eliminates ambiguity anxiety," "Provides narrative closure."]

---

**Requirements:**

- Every script confirms what was discussed so customers know their issue is on record
- Next steps are crystal clear; avoid vague promises like "soon" or "shortly"—use specific timelines
- Scripts stay under 4 sentences to respect customer time
- Language feels human, not corporate or bot-like
- Never use "Is there anything else?" when issues remain unresolved
- Eliminate hollow phrases like "We appreciate your business" or standalone "Have a great day!"
- No academic theory—focus on practical application under real-time pressure
- Visual separation between cards for mid-conversation scanning
- Scripts written in paragraph form, not bullets
```

# Analyze Closing Costs

## 簡介

The Analyze Closing Costs prompt is a free AI prompt that produces a complete buyer's closing preparation guide covering cost breakdowns, required documentation, and property handover checklists for residential real estate transactions. This closing costs prompt for ChatGPT walks buyers through the often-misunderstood 2-4% closing expense range beyond agent commissions, itemizing title insurance, escrow fees, recording fees, transfer taxes, prepaid property taxes, homeowner's insurance, HOA dues, inspection fees, appraisal fees, and lender charges with estimated ranges for each category. It runs on ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, and Grok, transforming a single {{property-details}} variable into a structured guide that explains why each fee exists, lists all paperwork to gather, organizes physical items like keys and garage openers, and maps preparation steps to a pre-closing timeline. Real estate agents, buyers, and transaction coordinators use it to eliminate last-minute surprises and ensure smooth closings. Reach for this prompt when you need to prepare a first-time buyer, answer "what do I owe at closing?" questions, or build a transaction checklist that covers financial, documentation, and handover obligations in one document. ● Itemizes every closing cost category with plain-language explanations of what each fee covers and why it exists. ● Provides a physical handover checklist grouping keys, access codes, warranties, manuals, and security items by category. ● Generates a day-by-day timeline mapping wire transfer deadlines, final walkthrough windows, and document review periods. ● Includes critical questions to ask your closing agent and a scannable final verification checklist with bold warnings for commonly forgotten items. ## Prompt

```
## Role
You are a real estate closing specialist with deep expertise in residential transactions, focused on demystifying closing costs, documentation, and handover requirements to prevent last-minute surprises.

## Task
Create a comprehensive closing preparation guide that helps the buyer anticipate all costs, gather required documentation, and prepare physical items for handover.

## Context
Closing costs typically range from 2-4% of the purchase price, excluding agent commission—a figure many buyers misunderstand. Multiple parties demand simultaneous payment while documentation and physical handover obligations pile up under time pressure.

{{property-details}}

## Output
Provide a structured preparation document with these sections:

**1. Financial Preparation**
- Break down the 2-4% closing cost estimate into specific fee categories: title insurance, escrow fees, recording fees, transfer taxes, prepaid items (property taxes, homeowner's insurance, HOA dues), inspection fees, appraisal fees, lender charges
- Show estimated cost ranges for each category based on the purchase price
- Highlight fees commonly misunderstood or overlooked
- Explain why each fee exists in plain language

**2. Documentation Requirements**
- List all paperwork to gather and verify before closing
- Explain what each document proves and why it's needed
- Note which items the closing agent provides vs. what the buyer must bring

**3. Physical Items Checklist**
Group by category:
- Access items: all keys, garage/gate openers, mailbox keys
- Security items: alarm codes, keypad combinations, safe codes
- Documentation that stays with property: warranties, manuals, HOA documents, appliance guides

**4. Critical Questions for Your Closing Agent**
- Specific questions to ask that eliminate surprises
- What to request in advance: final closing disclosure, wire instructions, settlement statement
- Confirmation points for day-of logistics

**5. Timeline**
- Map each preparation step to specific days before closing
- Identify critical deadlines: wire transfer cutoffs, final walkthrough window, document review periods

**6. Final Verification Checklist**
- Scannable checkbox format covering all financial, documentation, and physical items
- **Bold warnings** for commonly forgotten items

Use bullet points for lists, clear headings, and simple cost breakdowns. Translate legal terminology into accessible language. Focus on actionable steps the buyer can execute immediately.
```

# Trademark Cease-And-Desist Letter Generator

## 簡介

The Trademark Cease-And-Desist Letter Generator is a free AI prompt that drafts legally structured cease-and-desist letters for trademark attorneys protecting intellectual property rights. This trademark cease-and-desist prompt for ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, and Grok produces a complete business letter that identifies trademark ownership with registration details, establishes chronological priority and first-use dates, documents infringing activities with supporting evidence, and analyzes likelihood of confusion using circuit-specific factors including mark similarity, goods and services relatedness, trade channel overlap, and consumer sophistication. Attorneys use it to send formal demand letters that balance legal authority with negotiation strategy, whether protecting brand names, logos, or trade dress from unauthorized use. Reach for this prompt when you need to draft a formal infringement notice that clearly articulates trademark rights, demonstrates confusion risk, specifies cessation demands with deadlines, and outlines consequences while leaving room for settlement. ● Establishes trademark ownership and priority with registration numbers, filing dates, and classification details to create legal foundation. ● Analyzes likelihood of confusion using relevant circuit factors and documents specific infringing activities with evidence. ● Structures demands for cessation with reasonable deadlines and outlines non-compliance consequences while maintaining negotiation flexibility. ● Adapts tone from firm to collaborative based on your strategic goals and formats output as a complete business letter ready for attorney review. ## Prompt

```
## Role

You are a trademark attorney drafting a cease-and-desist letter that establishes priority, demonstrates likelihood of confusion, and demands specific remedies while maintaining strategic positioning for negotiation.

## Task

Draft a comprehensive trademark cease-and-desist letter in proper business letter format following ABA best practices. The letter must:

- Clearly identify trademark ownership with registration details (numbers, filing dates, classification)
- Chronologically establish priority and first use dates
- Detail the infringing activities with specific evidence
- Analyze likelihood of confusion using relevant circuit factors: mark similarity, goods/services relatedness, trade channel overlap, consumer sophistication, and any actual confusion instances
- State clear demands for cessation with specific deadlines
- Outline consequences for non-compliance
- Close professionally, leaving room for reasonable resolution
```

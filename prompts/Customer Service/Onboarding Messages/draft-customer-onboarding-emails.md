# Customer Onboarding Email Prompt for Welcome Sequences

## 簡介

The Customer Onboarding Email Prompt for Welcome Sequences is a free AI prompt that writes retention-focused welcome emails for SaaS companies, course creators, and subscription businesses during the critical first 10 minutes after signup. This customer onboarding email prompt for ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, and Grok produces a complete welcome email with a curiosity-driven subject line and a four-part body structure: a human greeting that breaks automated patterns, confirmation of what the customer now has access to, a single friction-free next action, and a personal close that makes support feel accessible. You supply three variables (product name, value proposition, and desired first action), and the prompt returns an email under 200 words that addresses buyer's remorse, analysis paralysis, and distraction in the vulnerable window between signup and first real use. Reach for this prompt when you need onboarding copy that feels like a friend texting next steps, not a corporation processing users through a funnel. ● Writes subject lines that create curiosity or immediate value while avoiding generic "Welcome to" patterns. ● Structures the email around the emotional state of someone who just committed but hasn't experienced value yet. ● Enforces one clear call-to-action and removes feature dumps, jargon, and multiple competing next steps. ● Produces warm, quietly confident tone that sounds like a competent colleague, not corporate marketing. ## Prompt

```
## Role
You are an onboarding copywriter who specializes in the critical first 10 minutes after signup—the window that predicts long-term retention. Your writing feels like a friend texting exactly what to do next, not a corporation processing users through a funnel.

## Task
Write a welcome email that makes a new customer feel seen, reassured, and gently pulled toward one clear next action. The user just signed up but hasn't taken their first real action. They're in the danger zone: excited enough to commit but vulnerable to buyer's remorse, distraction, or analysis paralysis.

## Context
Before writing, consider:
- What emotion are they feeling right now?
- What doubt might be creeping in?
- What's the smallest action that creates the biggest momentum shift?
- How would I write this if it were going to my best friend?

**Product/Service:** {{product-name}}  
**Primary Value:** {{value-proposition}}  
**Desired First Action:** {{first-action}}

## Output
Provide the complete email in standard email format:

**Subject Line:** [One compelling subject line that creates curiosity or immediate value—avoid "Welcome to" or "Thanks for signing up"]

**Email Body:**  
[Full email text organized in natural paragraphs that flow like an actual email. Must accomplish four objectives in under 200 words:

1. **Human Greeting (15-25 words):** Break the automated message pattern instantly. Acknowledge them as an individual.

2. **Confirmation (30-50 words):** Eliminate confusion about what just happened. Validate their decision and confirm what they now have access to in plain language.

3. **Single Next Step (40-60 words):** Give ONE specific, achievable action. Remove all friction and decision fatigue. Make it feel like the natural next move, not homework.

4. **Personal Close (20-35 words):** Make support feel accessible. End with something that sounds like a real person signing off.

Include the call-to-action naturally embedded in the copy. Indicate where a link/button would go. End with an appropriate sign-off and sender name (e.g., "Sarah from [Company]").]

**Avoid:**
- Generic corporate phrases ("We're thrilled," "Don't hesitate," "Dear Valued Customer")
- Multiple CTAs or feature dumps
- Jargon, buzzwords, overly formal or casual extremes
- More than one exclamation point in the entire email

**Focus on:**
- The emotional state of someone who just committed but hasn't experienced value yet
- Making the next action feel inevitable and easy
- Warm, human, quietly confident tone—like a competent colleague
- Specificity over vagueness
- Building trust through clarity, not claims
```

# Value Proposition Framework Builder

## 簡介

The Value Proposition Framework Builder is a free AI prompt that creates research-backed positioning frameworks for businesses defining their market message and competitive advantage. This value proposition prompt for ChatGPT takes a business and audience description and produces an 8-section strategic document covering customer psychology (Point A struggles, Point B goals, obstacles, and magnets), product positioning, feature priorities, competitive alternatives, and differentiation tactics. It runs on ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini, simulating the output of weeks of user research and competitive analysis. Marketing teams use it to align product roadmaps with messaging; founders use it to clarify positioning before launch; strategists use it to audit existing value propositions against competitor moves. Reach for this prompt when you need a structured, psychologically grounded positioning framework rather than generic taglines or surface-level benefits. ● Surfaces psychological Point A pain and Point B aspiration for a named persona, grounding all messaging in real customer motivation. ● Identifies obstacles that repel prospects and magnets that accelerate purchase decisions, informing conversion optimization and campaign creative. ● Maps the "nemesis" competitor and proposes three niche-down differentiation angles across product, pricing, marketing, or design. ● Outputs transformation positioning, three priority features to build, and a cohesive framework that connects audience research to product and marketing strategy. ## Prompt

```
## Role
You are a strategic marketing analyst specializing in value proposition development through competitive and audience research.

## Task
Create a detailed Value Proposition framework for the business described below. This framework will guide marketing campaigns, product development, and messaging. Base all insights on deep audience psychology, competitive positioning, and market dynamics.

## Context
{{business-and-audience}}

## Output
Deliver the Value Proposition using this exact 8-component structure:

**Customer Insights (written from the perspective of one named persona):**

- **Point A**: What one specific irritating problem is bothering this persona right now? What negative consequences impact their life? Describe the struggle they hope to escape. Introduce the persona by name here. One paragraph.

- **Point B**: What one specific ideal future does this persona want to achieve? What benefits do they seek? Describe their goal. One paragraph.

- **Obstacles**: What 3 aspects of the product or marketing might push this persona away—moments that irritate them or nudge them back to the status quo? Bullet list with in-depth explanations.

- **Magnets**: What 3 aspects of the product or marketing will attract this persona—moments that build trust, engage, and accelerate purchase decisions? Bullet list with in-depth explanations.

**Product Insights:**

- **Transformation**: How should the product positioning be communicated to attract ideal customers? Write one 2-sentence paragraph: a positioning statement followed by an explanation of how it connects product to audience.

- **Features**: What 3 advanced product features should be built or enhanced to deliver the promised transformation? Bullet list.

- **Nemesis**: What type of product is the biggest competitor? Describe why this alternative is attractive to the ideal customer. One in-depth paragraph.

- **Differentiation**: What 3 niche-down ideas (product, pricing, marketing, design, etc.) would make this offering more unique versus the nemesis? Bullet list.

**Quality standards:**
- Be specific. Choose one representative persona and one coherent strategy. Avoid generic platitudes.
- Go deep. Reveal true psychological motives as if drawing from 30 user interviews and weeks of research, not surface observations.
- Never return trivial advice like "user-friendly interface" or "affordable pricing" that applies to any business. Deliver insights personalized to this specific situation.
- Keep Features, Differentiation, and Magnets distinct—they're related but must offer different angles.
- Make insights actionable for marketing and product decisions.
- Write concisely in simple sentences. Use a matter-of-fact, straightforward, engaging tone. Avoid salesy consultant language.
- Make informed assumptions to connect product, audience, and competitive landscape holistically.
```

# Text Sentiment Analysis Tutorial Prompt

## 簡介

The Text Sentiment Analysis Tutorial Prompt is a free AI prompt that produces structured, educational guides teaching users how to identify emotional tone in written content. This sentiment analysis prompt for ChatGPT walks through a complete methodology: reading for context, spotting sentiment-bearing words, analyzing sentence structure, detecting sarcasm and irony, gauging intensity, and synthesizing an overall classification. It runs on ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, and Grok, transforming any text passage into a teaching moment by breaking down the evaluation process into clear steps with examples. Writers, marketers, customer-success teams, and students use it to understand audience perception, decode feedback, and improve communication analysis skills. Reach for this prompt when you need to train others on sentiment evaluation or want to build a transparent, repeatable framework for interpreting tone in reviews, social posts, surveys, or support tickets. ● Breaks sentiment analysis into seven actionable steps, from initial reading to final classification ● Explains how to detect sarcasm, irony, and mixed signals that flip or complicate surface meaning ● Offers strategies for handling ambiguity and provides guidance on weighting competing emotional cues ● Formats output with numbered steps, bold key terms, and short paragraphs for maximum clarity ## Prompt

```
## Role
You are an expert guide teaching sentiment analysis—the process of identifying whether text expresses positive, negative, or neutral emotion.

## Task
Provide a clear, step-by-step tutorial that enables the user to accurately determine the sentiment of {{text-to-analyze}}. Break down the process into manageable steps, illustrate key concepts with examples, and address common pitfalls.

{{additional-context}}

## Process

### Introduction
- Define sentiment analysis and explain why it matters for understanding audience perception, customer feedback, and communication effectiveness
- Preview the systematic approach below

### Step-by-Step Instructions

1. **Read thoroughly**
   - Absorb the complete text before judging
   - Note the overall tone and context (e.g., a product review will use emotive language to signal satisfaction or frustration)

2. **Identify sentiment-bearing words and phrases**
   - Positive indicators: "fantastic," "delighted," "impressive"
   - Negative indicators: "terrible," "frustrated," "disappointing"
   - Distinguish these from neutral descriptors that carry no emotional weight

3. **Analyze structure and emphasis**
   - Sentence order affects impact: "While the service was good, the food was terrible" emphasizes the negative
   - Conjunctions like "but" and "however" often signal which sentiment dominates

4. **Track patterns and repetition**
   - Recurring sentiment intensifies the message: "boring, tedious, and a complete waste of time" compounds negativity

5. **Detect sarcasm, irony, and tonal shifts**
   - These devices flip surface meaning: "Well, that was a fantastic waste of time" is negative despite "fantastic"
   - Look for context clues that signal inversion

6. **Gauge intensity**
   - Mild: "I liked it"
   - Strong: "I absolutely adored it"
   - Intensity informs confidence in your classification

7. **Synthesize the overall sentiment**
   - Weigh all evidence to classify as positive, negative, or neutral
   - If genuinely mixed or ambiguous, label it neutral and note the competing signals

### Handling Ambiguity
- Mixed-sentiment texts require you to identify which tone predominates or acknowledge balance
- Factor in the author's intent, audience, and situational context
- When uncertain, cite specific passages supporting each possible interpretation

### Conclusion
Sentiment analysis improves with practice. Review your classifications, compare with others' interpretations, and refine your sensitivity to linguistic nuance over time.

## Output Format
- Use bullet points and numbered steps for clarity
- **Bold** key terms and examples
- Keep paragraphs short and well-spaced for readability
```

# Live Chat Greeting Scripts for Customer Support

## 簡介

The Live Chat Greeting Scripts for Customer Support is a free AI prompt that creates intent-aware opening messages for support teams looking to cut chat abandonment in the first 30 seconds of contact. This live chat greeting prompt for ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, and Grok produces a table of 10 opening greetings across five contact reasons (billing issues, technical problems, product questions, complaints, and general inquiries), each with a casual and a formal variation. Every greeting acknowledges the customer's likely intent immediately, sets clear wait-time or next-step expectations, and closes with an open-ended question - all under 50 words and without generic filler language. Support teams use it to replace corporate templates that drive customers away, tailoring tone to brand voice and typical customer profile. Reach for this prompt when you manage high-volume live chat and need greetings that make customers feel understood from the first second, matching their energy and reducing drop-off before the conversation begins. ● Covers five high-frequency contact reasons with two tone variations each, so agents can match customer energy in real time. ● Eliminates generic phrases and filler language that signal automated responses and increase bounce rates. ● Outputs greetings under 50 words that acknowledge intent, set expectations, and invite detail-sharing in one move. ● Includes guidance notes on when to select casual versus formal variations based on message content and customer signals. ## Prompt

```
## Role

You are a customer experience strategist specializing in first-contact chat optimization. You design opening messages that reduce abandonment in the critical first 30 seconds by immediately acknowledging customer intent and setting clear expectations.

## Context

Customer support teams face high chat abandonment rates when generic greetings fail to make customers feel understood within the first few seconds. Frustrated customers arrive ready to leave if the opening message doesn't signal they've reached the right place. Corporate templates and filler language increase drop-off because they delay acknowledgment of customer needs.

## Task

Create 10 opening chat greetings (2 variations × 5 common contact reasons) tailored to {{business-context}}.

Address these five contact reasons:
- Billing issues
- Technical problems
- Product questions
- Complaints
- General inquiries

For each reason, write two variations:
- **Variation A (Casual)**: conversational, warm, mirrors relaxed customer energy
- **Variation B (Formal)**: professional, precise, mirrors serious customer tone

Each greeting must:
1. Acknowledge the customer's likely intent in the opening sentence
2. Set clear expectations about wait time or next steps
3. Close with an open-ended question that invites detail-sharing
4. Stay under 50 words
5. Eliminate generic filler ("How can I help you today?", "Thank you for reaching out", "I'd be happy to assist")
6. Avoid generic openings ("Hi there", "Hello, welcome")
7. Use maximum one exclamation mark per greeting

Adapt tone and terminology to match {{brand-voice}} and {{typical-customer}}.

## Output

Deliver as a table:

| Chat Reason | Variation A (Casual) | Variation B (Formal) |
|-------------|---------------------|---------------------|
| ... | ... | ... |

Below the table, provide a brief guidance note (3–4 sentences) explaining when to select casual vs. formal variations based on customer signals, message content, or communication style indicators.
```

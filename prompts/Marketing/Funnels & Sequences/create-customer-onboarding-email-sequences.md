# Customer Onboarding Email Sequence Generator

## 簡介

The Customer Onboarding Email Sequence Generator is a free AI prompt that creates behavior-driven onboarding sequences designed to move new users from skeptical signups to active, engaged customers within 14 days. This customer onboarding email sequence prompt for ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, and Grok produces five timed emails that follow a narrative arc: a day-zero welcome that reduces anxiety and provides one clear first step, a day-two quick-win email that delivers early accomplishment, a day-five core-value message connecting features to real outcomes, day-nine social proof validating the decision through others' success, and a day-14 check-in that opens genuine human connection. Each email stays under 180 words, includes subject lines and preview text, and follows behavioral psychology principles that engineer small, compounding victories rather than overwhelming users with feature lists. Marketing teams use it to reduce churn during the critical first two weeks, SaaS founders deploy it to improve activation rates, and customer success managers adapt it to guide users toward their first meaningful product outcome. Reach for this prompt when you need to transform new signups into active users by guiding them through a structured journey that feels like a patient mentor, not a marketing blast. ● Structures five emails across a 14-day window with specific timing and purpose for each touchpoint ● Writes subject lines, preview text, and body copy under 180 words in a conversational, mentor-like tone ● Tailors messaging to your specific product, target customer, and the core outcome you want users to achieve ● Includes exactly one clear call-to-action per email and avoids feature dumps or hard selling ## Prompt

```
## Role

You are a lifecycle email strategist who designs onboarding sequences that guide new users to their first meaningful outcome. Your approach is rooted in behavioral psychology: onboarding succeeds when it engineers small, compounding victories rather than explaining features. Write emails that feel like a patient mentor, not a marketing department.

## Context

A new user has just signed up but hasn't yet experienced the product's core value. They are in a critical 14-day window where they will either become active or abandon the product. Most onboarding sequences fail by overwhelming users with features instead of guiding them to one clear first outcome.

## Task

Write a five-email onboarding sequence that transforms a skeptical signup into an engaged user who understands the product's value in their specific situation. Each email builds on the previous one, creating a narrative arc across two weeks.

**Sequence structure:**
- **Email 1 (Day 0):** Welcome — reduce anxiety, provide one clear first step
- **Email 2 (Day 2):** Quick Win — deliver an early accomplishment
- **Email 3 (Day 5):** Core Value — connect features to real outcomes
- **Email 4 (Day 9):** Social Proof — validate their decision through others' success
- **Email 5 (Day 14):** Check-In — open a genuine human connection point

**Requirements:**
- Each email under 180 words
- Warm, conversational tone like a knowledgeable friend
- Exactly one clear call-to-action per email
- Subject lines create curiosity or promise value
- Preview text (under 90 characters) complements the subject line
- Short paragraphs (2-3 sentences max), natural language
- Acknowledge the user's specific context, not generic benefits
- No feature dumps, jargon, or hard selling
- Final email genuinely invites response

**Product context:**
- Product/service: {{product-service}}
- Target customer: {{target-customer}}
- Core value outcome: {{core-value-outcome}}

## Output

Present each email as a complete unit:

**Email [Number] (Timing): [Purpose]**

**Subject Line:** [The subject line]

**Preview Text:** [Under 90 characters]

**Email Body:**
[Complete email text with natural paragraph breaks]

[Clear CTA]

---

Separate each email with a line break. Write all five emails in sequence, maintaining consistent voice and building narrative momentum from first contact through day 14.
```
